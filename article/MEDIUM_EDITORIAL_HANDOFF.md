### Two corrections worth recording

**Quote fidelity was being reported as a fidelity result and is not one.** The generator is instructed not to reproduce review text verbatim, and across all 100 explanations it never does: `generation.quotes` is empty in every row and no explanation contains a quotation mark. The deterministic check therefore has a denominator of zero. Earlier drafts reported "exact short-quote fidelity 95%" and then "100%" as though fragments had been matched against sources. The article now reports the row as "quotation contract respected" and states plainly that every reference to review evidence is a paraphrase, which is why entailment rather than string matching carries those claims, and lists paraphrase verification as an unverified property in the limits section.

**The worked example was sourced from the wrong file.** An earlier draft of this revision used a Jack in the Box case drawn from the twelve-case Hit@10 illustration set while captioning it as one of the 100 audited recommendations. Figure 1 and the worked-example text now use sample 2 of `regenerated_entity_v2`, Coche Valley Dessert Cafe, which is in the audited sample.

### GraphRAG: patch rather than rebuild

The decision on the explanation layer is to patch the evidence serializer and the judging rubric and keep the retrieval design. Retrieval is not what is failing: held-out safety and citation resolution are both at 100%. What fails is that the serialized evidence does not record entity ownership, and that the rubric folds completeness into entailment. Both are changes to the bundle builder and the judging prompt, not to the model or the subgraph assembly, and both are prerequisites to the two selected runs. They are listed as such in the request table.

### Part 4: reading the judge reasons

Revision 2 reported that 48% of explanations score 1 or 2 on entailment and treated that as the rate of unsupported generation. Reading the 48 written reasons behind those scores shows it is an upper bound that mixes three different things.

- **Roughly half, 22 to 26 of 48 depending on borderline cases, turn on an entity-attribution dispute** that the evidence schema cannot settle. Every review excerpt in every bundle is scaffolded "A training-safe reviewer rated it 5 stars and wrote: ...", and the node's own wording never names the restaurant the review belongs to. In a minority of bundles the quoted text happens to mention the name, but that is an accident of what the reviewer wrote. 47 of the 48 low-entailment explanations cite at least one review node. In one case the judge diagnoses the defect itself, objecting that "[Q1] does not explicitly state which restaurant the quote refers to, only that a 'training-safe reviewer' rated 'it' 5 stars". In several others it resolves the ambiguity the other way and asserts something the bundle does not contain, for example: that the evidence "explicitly states these reviews are for Big Cheese Pizza" when the nodes name no restaurant, and that an explanation attributes a review "to 'the diner'" when the explanation says "A reviewer noted".
- **7 of 48 record no unsupported claim at all and still score 1 or 2**, penalizing what the explanation left out. Four of those concern a contradicting attribute, the clearest being a restaurant value score of 0.5 against a user value preference of 1.0, where the judge writes that the explanation "is factually supported but lacks completeness". That is a rubric mismatch rather than a generation failure, and a real signal about compression: roughly fourteen evidence nodes become two sentences and nothing requires a contradicting node to survive.
- **8 of 48 are criticized for generic wording.**
- **The residual is genuine calibration failure**, which is what the figure was meant to measure. It is smaller than 48% and the current audit cannot say how much smaller.

These counts are a considered classification of model-written prose, not a measurement, and the article says so. An earlier automated pass over the same reasons returned 27, 13 and 13 for the second, third and fourth groups; independent re-reading showed those regexes over-matched, and the corrected figures are used throughout.

The article now states this directly, reports 48% as an upper bound, hedges the split as a reading rather than a measurement, and carries a "Mitigations" section. Two cheap fixes follow, both listed as prerequisites in the request table: name the entity on every evidence node, and split entailment from completeness in the rubric. The article's claims were weakened accordingly (see below).

The activity-stratum paragraph was cut. At 25 cases per stratum the max-minus-min spreads run from 1.1 to 2.2 standard errors of the difference, which does not support the comparison.

## Series-level narrative (unchanged)

- A local LLM consolidates heterogeneous semantic extraction into one schema; conventional NLP could reproduce the signals through multiple specialized components.
- LLM-feature benefit is asymmetric across architectures and is reported as an observed interaction, not a causal message-passing explanation.
- Explicit proximity adds substantially more than the semantic bundle even though spatial CBG edges are present in KGAT-SAL.
- GraphRAG explains a fixed result after ranking. It provides auditable evidence paths but does not expose the GNN's causal computation.

No numerical comparison is made with the archived exploratory chart because its candidate universe, embedding size, and checkpoint-selection protocol differ.
